Optimizations

What made Manyan fast, what was tried and gave nothing, and how to measure. Each entry gives wall-clock seconds on the bench specs (arm64, best of 5) before and after; benchmark.md has the current comparison with TLC.

Measuring

  • bench/quick.sh [-workers N] OLD NEW: the best of 5 runs of each engine on each bench spec, side by side. Compare binaries built from two commits, at the same time; nothing else may run meanwhile (a stray background job once hid a result).
  • sample PID 3 -file out.sample on a build with -O2 -g, run on a model big enough to sample (counters with Max = 8: 4.78M states). Self samples point at the hot function; to split an inlined one, build a copy with __attribute__((noinline)) on its parts.
  • To count work instead of time, build a copy that counts eval calls by kind and arena allocations by caller (__builtin_return_address). For counters: 148 evaluations and 53 allocations per state expanded.
  • make bench re-measures benchmark.md against TLC with the profile-guided build; do it before a release.

Where the time goes now

Counters, one worker: about 60% evaluating (148 tree nodes per state at about 7 ns each), 35% keeping states (fingerprint lookup 16%, encoding, records), 6% decoding. Closure: a third in set membership of tuples. Two workers reach 1.6x one; the parallel path costs about 20% more work (claims, encoding twice, a second lookup when merging).

The next large step for evaluation is fewer, cheaper nodes: resolving names to slots, and fusing common shapes (f[x] with f a variable and x a bound name, comparisons of integers) at resolve time.

Open: where TLC gains more from workers

Examples' models where Manyan is least ahead of TLC (4 workers each) are ones with a large serial part, not a slow evaluator.

  • AllocatorImplementation (TLC 3.44 s, Manyan 1.38 s; Manyan 1.32 s with one worker). Its property SchedAllocator is checked on the state graph after the search, in one process: about half the samples are in scc_search, evaluating [Sched!Next]_vars on each of 64414 edges and the fairness ENABLEDs of Sched!Liveness on each state. TLC evaluates these as each worker expands a state (LiveCheck.addNextState). Kept for each edge, the atoms are now evaluated once an edge, not 32 times (above), and the workers evaluate them before the check, each on the edges of its states: 0.75 → 0.52 s with 4 workers. Left in one process are the 115 fairness conditions on each state and edge: the check makes 570424 ENABLED evaluations, 468217 of them distinct, and 315589 steps, all distinct, of the 2.0 million and 7.4 million there are. Evaluating all of them in the workers would be about four times the work, so it would take components checked in parallel, not a fill. subst_index (above) took 3.6% off the evaluation itself. Since 0.11.0 a property is checked by its possible error models (PEMs), searched lazily, one at a time; filling every atom of every step in the workers first doubled the time with 4 workers (0.48 → 0.96 s). Instead the workers each search error models of the product, filling the shared atom cache as they go, and the search goes on in order from what they found: 0.48 → 0.24 s with 4 workers, 0.46 → 0.21 s with 8. Each search still took the <>[] conditions of its error model on every edge itself, 60% of its evaluations, and 7 error models make two rounds on 4 workers. The workers now evaluate those first, by state: 0.25 → 0.22 s with 4 workers, 0.22 → 0.20 s with 8. Not where the offers (below) took them already, nor for a group of one error model, nor of state atoms alone: forking costs more there (EWD840 0.006 → 0.009 s, the quick bench's liveness +53% with 8 workers).
  • ReadersWriters, MCLiveWriteThroughCache. A product of more than 8 error models first computes what each component offers them, on one process: 0.25 s of ReadersWriters' 0.29 s with 4 workers. The components are found first, by a search that evaluates nothing, and the workers evaluate the offers' conditions in them, by state: ReadersWriters 0.25 → 0.12 s, MCLiveWriteThroughCache 0.19 → 0.09 s with 4 workers. Only above 100000 evaluations: below, the fork costs more (ElevatorLivenessMedium +26%, LiveHourClock +43%).
  • The quick bench's liveness. 0.17 s with one worker and 0.16 with eight: 0.14 s was Served, five groups of one error model each, a tableau each, checked in turn by one process. The workers now check the groups, each in one worker, and the search checks again in order those violated or not known: 0.168 → 0.097 s with 8 workers, SimpleAllocator 0.122 → 0.026, MCAlternatingBit 0.046 → 0.012. Not for two groups, which lose the workers' search inside each (TestGraphs +32%, EWD840 +66%); nor more workers forked than a level has batches, a fork costing each (CoffeeCan100Beans +41% with 8 workers, now +9%). Left: groups that share their atoms evaluate them in each worker, where in turn the first fills them for the rest (CoffeeCan100Beans +9%, MCLiveInternalMemory +8% with 4 workers, a few milliseconds); the first group checked first, by the search, made those faster and MCAlternatingBit +94%, DijkstraMutex +60%.
